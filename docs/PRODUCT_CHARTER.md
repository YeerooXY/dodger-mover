# Product charter

## Vision

Create a side-profile combat-platformer that feels like discovering an unusually polished Flash game: immediate controls, readable silhouettes, exaggerated impact, surprising environmental interactions, and enough combat expression to support mastery.

`Dodger Mover` is a working title, not a final brand decision.

## Player promise

Within thirty seconds, the player should be moving and hitting something. Within five minutes, they should discover at least one interaction they did not expect: launching an enemy onto another platform, continuing a combo in the air, using a drop as an attack, or turning the arena layout into an advantage.

## Design pillars

1. **Impact before content.** Hit timing, reaction, sound, displacement, and control responsiveness matter more than move count.
2. **Traversal is combat.** Platforms create offensive choices rather than pauses between fights.
3. **Readable depth.** A new player can improvise immediately; mastery comes from timing, direction, positioning, and cancels.
4. **Flash-game directness.** Fast startup, short sessions, strong personality, and very little ceremony.
5. **Authored surprise.** Compact encounters should produce memorable situations without needing enormous content volume.

## Initial audience

- Players nostalgic for browser-era action games.
- Players who enjoy expressive brawlers but do not want fighting-game onboarding overhead.
- Keyboard and controller players on Windows.

## First releasable target

A polished 20-30 minute Windows demo containing:

- One coherent environment with meaningful verticality.
- A compact but expressive player moveset.
- At least three enemy pressures that combine well.
- One escalation or boss encounter.
- A scoring or style reason to replay.
- A clear ending and a reliable restart loop.

## Non-goals until the core is proven

- Online multiplayer.
- Procedural campaign generation.
- Large inventory, crafting, or skill-tree systems.
- Cinematic story production.
- Mobile controls.
- Final monetization design.

## Definition of fun for the first prototype

S001 succeeds when the player voluntarily repeats the room to try a different sequence, not merely when every planned feature exists.
